Field Notes: 01 · Introduction to Prompt Engineering

Big Idea: A language model produces a continuation of the information it receives. Prompt engineering is the practice of making that continuation useful, relevant, and reliable.

The Central Question

Imagine giving a language model the words: "The most important discovery of the experiment was…". Many continuations are possible. The model could finish a scientific report, invent a dramatic story, or produce a short summary. The words surrounding the request determine which kind of continuation is likely.

That is the starting point of prompt engineering: designing the instructions, examples, and context so that the model's output serves a particular purpose. In an application, prompt engineering also means deciding what information to retrieve, how to structure a conversation, and what to do with the result.

1. How Did We Get Here?

The path from early language models to conversational assistants is a sequence of solutions to information-processing bottlenecks:

Era Key development What changed?
1940s onward Statistical language models & Markov approaches Predict text from a strictly limited preceding context.
2014 Sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models Translate or transform sequences using an encoder and decoder.
2015 Attention in neural sequence models Allow a decoder to access relevant portions of input rather than relying on one compressed vector.
2017 The Transformer architecture Use attention as the central mechanism for representing relationships across sequences.
2018 GPT: Generative Pre-trained Transformer Pretrain a transformer text generator, then adapt it to useful downstream tasks.
GPT-2 → GPT-3 → GPT-4 Increasingly capable language models Prompting becomes an important way to perform tasks without retraining models for each one.

Remember: The transformation was not from “predicting words” to an entirely different principle. It was from simple predictions with limited context to powerful context-sensitive prediction.

2. The Original Challenge: A Sentence is Not a Single Vector

Early seq2seq translation relied on an encoder compressing an entire sentence into a fixed-size thought vector, which a decoder used to generate output. The bottleneck: compressing long paragraphs into one vector lost crucial qualifications, names, and grammatical links.

Without attention With attention
One summary vector carries all input information. Decoder consults a collection of source representations.
Details disappear in long sequences. Different source words become important at different output steps.
A narrow information bottleneck. Flexible, dynamic access to context.

3. The Three Limitations Worth Remembering

  • A language model is not automatically a search engine. It can produce plausible text without having retrieved or verified a source.
  • Context is finite. A model processes only the content available within its context window. Selecting and compressing information is an engineering decision.
  • Fluent does not mean correct. A well-written explanation can still contain factual or logical errors. Verification matters most when errors have consequences.

The Training Pipeline: SFT → Preference → RLHF

Base language models are conditioned on raw corpora. Aligned chat models go through four distinct stages:

  • Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT): Curated human demonstrations show what high-quality assistant replies look like.
  • Human Preference Comparisons: Humans rank candidate responses (Candidate A vs B vs C).
  • Reward Modeling: A neural reward model is trained to predict which responses humans score highest.
  • Reinforcement Learning (PPO): The policy is optimized against the reward model.

The Four Requirements of an Effective Prompt

  1. A familiar format: Structure like a recognizable document or dialogue (The Little Red Riding Hood principle).
  2. Enough relevant information: Supply what is necessary; omit clutter.
  3. A clear direction: Tell the model what to accomplish and how to begin.
  4. A controlled stopping point: Define the output boundary explicitly.

Feedforward Pipeline: 4 Stages

Stage What happens? Example
1. Context retrieval Collect useful information. Read the relevant support article.
2. Snippetization Split long material into manageable excerpts. Extract the paragraph about fan noise.
3. Prioritization Rank pieces by usefulness and relevance. Device-specific instructions before generic tips.
4. Prompt assembly Combine task, constraints, and context. Build the final structured troubleshooting request.
